**Date:** 2026-05-22  
**Confidence:** Medium

---

## Competitive Alternatives

If Saarthi didn't exist, users would:

**Students:**
- Ask ChatGPT / Gemini (generic, no Indian exam context, slow, ₹1,750/month for Plus)
- Watch YouTube / PhysicsWallah videos (passive, can't interact, no personalisation)
- Post in WhatsApp/Telegram groups (slow, depends on who's online)
- Hire a tutor (₹500–₹2,000/hour, unavailable at midnight)
- Do nothing — stay stuck, move on, get it wrong on exam day

**Founders:**
- Pay a CA ₹5,000–₹20,000 for basic compliance advice
- Google it (unreliable, outdated, requires expertise to interpret)
- Ask in Reddit/LinkedIn (slow, inconsistent quality)
- Buy YC content (US-centric, misses India-specific nuances)

## Unique Attributes

What Saarthi has that alternatives don't:

| Attribute | Why it's unique |
|-----------|----------------|
| India-exam-tuned AI | Trained prompts that know NCERT, PYQ patterns, specific exam formats (JEE vs NEET vs UPSC differ radically in question style) |
| Socratic methodology | No Indian platform asks guiding questions — all give answers. This is pedagogically superior and token-efficient. |
| Mistake DNA | No platform diagnoses error type patterns across sessions |
| PYQ Pattern Predictor | Requires 10-15 years of paper curation — not a chatbot feature |
| India Stack Navigator | India-specific regulatory intelligence baked into structured AI output |
| Bharat Pricing Engine | India income distribution + competitive landscape built into pricing advice |
| Real-time speed (Groq) | Groq LPU = 500+ tokens/sec. Feels instant. Faster than GPT-4 for students on slow connections. |
| Zero cost to user (Free tier) | The comparison set all costs money or has a steep paywall |

## Value Created

What those attributes enable:

**For students:**
- Actually understanding concepts, not just copying answers → better exam performance
- Knowing your exact weakness → less wasted study time
- Having a study companion at 2am → reduced anxiety, consistent preparation
- Confidence from psychology coaching → fewer blank-mind moments on exam day

**For founders:**
- Avoiding costly compliance mistakes in Year 1 → saves ₹50,000–₹2,00,000 in professional fees
- Pricing correctly from Day 1 → fewer pivots, faster revenue
- Pitching the right investors → higher meeting conversion rates
- Having a roadmap → reducing the "I don't know what to do next" paralysis

## Target Market (Who Cares Most)

**Beachhead:** JEE/NEET aspirants in Class 11–12 in Tier 2/3 cities (Patna, Lucknow, Kota, Jaipur, Indore, Nagpur) who:
- Can't afford Unacademy premium
- Are already using YouTube + WhatsApp for study
- Have a smartphone but not a laptop
- Are 60–90 days from their exam

This segment:
- Has acute pain (exam is imminent)
- Has low cost-of-acquisition (Reddit, Telegram, WhatsApp groups)
- Will tell friends if it works (viral coefficient is high in tight exam prep communities)
- Will convert to paid if it demonstrably helps them

## Market Category

**Category we're creating:** "AI exam co-pilot" (not "EdTech platform," not "AI chatbot")

This framing matters because:
- "EdTech platform" competes with Byju's/PW — big, funded, branded
- "AI chatbot" competes with ChatGPT — impossible to beat
- "AI exam co-pilot" is new, positions Saarthi as a tool that helps you fight your exam (not replace your teacher)
- For founders: "India startup co-pilot" — same logic

**Taglines:**
- Primary: "Saarthi — Your AI co-pilot for India's hardest journeys"
- Student: "The AI that teaches you, not just tells you"
- Founder: "Navigate India's startup maze — AI that knows the rules"

---

## Flags

**Red Flags:**
- "Creating a new category" is expensive and slow. Consider explicitly positioning against ChatGPT: "ChatGPT for India's exams" is faster to understand, even if less elegant.

**Yellow Flags:**
- The two-sided platform positioning ("both students and founders") can confuse users. Each section should feel like it has its own identity even if it's one URL.
