## Persona 1: The Exam Warrior (Student)

**Name:** Arjun, 17, Patna  
**Segment:** JEE/NEET aspirant, Class 11–12

**Background:**
- Attends local coaching + watches PW/YouTube
- Has a ₹8,000 Android phone (Redmi/Realme), 4G data (Jio, 1.5GB/day)
- Family income: ₹40,000–₹80,000/month. Education is the family's biggest investment.
- Spends 10–14 hours/day studying, under extreme parental pressure

**Jobs to be Done:**
1. Understand a concept he can't grasp from videos
2. Practice questions and know WHY he got them wrong
3. Know which chapters to prioritise with 30 days to exam
4. Manage anxiety — "What if I fail? My whole life depends on this."
5. Get answers at 11pm when no teacher is available

**Pains:**
- Doubtnut gives video answers but doesn't explain the concept root cause
- ChatGPT is too general, expensive, and doesn't know NCERT/PYQ patterns
- Coaching teachers don't have time for individual doubt clearing
- Can't afford ₹15,000/year for Unacademy premium
- Data costs matter — heavy video streaming burns his daily quota

**Gains:**
- AI that explains like a friend, not a textbook
- Knowing exactly which 20% of topics will give 80% of marks
- Confidence going into the exam hall
- Something that works offline or on low bandwidth

**Language:** Hindi-comfortable, can read English. Prefers explanations in "Hinglish."

**Where to reach him:** PW Telegram groups, r/JEEPreparation (Reddit), YouTube comments on Alakh Pandey videos, WhatsApp study groups

## Persona 3: The First-Time Founder (Founder)

**Name:** Rahul, 26, Bangalore  
**Segment:** Ex-Zomato product manager, building a B2B SaaS for retail

**Background:**
- Quit job 2 months ago, bootstrapping
- Has an idea, a co-founder (tech), no customers yet
- Knows product but not compliance, fundraising, or India market pricing
- Budget: ₹1,50,000 personal savings (3 months runway)

**Jobs to be Done:**
1. Know what legal/compliance steps to take before launch
2. Price his SaaS correctly for Indian SMBs
3. Find the right angel investors in retail/B2B SaaS

**Gains:**
- Clarity on exactly what to do and in what order
- Confidence that he's not missing a compliance step
- Warm intros to the right investors
- Pitch feedback that simulates what an Indian angel would actually say

## Pain Hierarchy (Students)

1. **Critical:** Concept not understood (blocks exam performance directly)
2. **Critical:** Wrong answers — don't know why (can't improve without diagnosis)
3. **High:** Time management — studying wrong things
4. **High:** Exam anxiety — psychological readiness
5. **Medium:** Syllabus tracking — what's covered vs. pending
6. **Medium:** Answer writing quality (UPSC-specific)
7. **Low:** Community / peer comparison

## Pain Hierarchy (Founders)

1. **Critical:** Compliance confusion — afraid of getting in trouble unknowingly
2. **Critical:** Pricing wrong — charging too much or too little for India market
3. **High:** Can't find right investors
4. **High:** Pitch not resonating
5. **Medium:** Don't know what to build first
6. **Low:** Community / co-founder matching

## Buying Behaviour

**Students:**
- Decision maker: Student + parent jointly
- Price sensitivity: Extremely high. Will tolerate ads before paying. Need to see value before paying.
- Trial period needed: 7–14 days of free use
- Conversion trigger: "This helped me get one extra mark / understand one concept I'd been stuck on for weeks"

**Founders:**
- Decision maker: Founder alone
- Price sensitivity: Moderate. Will pay if it saves time worth more than the subscription cost.
- Trial period needed: 3–5 days
- Conversion trigger: "This saved me ₹20,000 in CA/legal fees" or "This intro led to a meeting"

## Flags

**Red Flags:**
- None

**Yellow Flags:**
- Priya (UPSC) needs answer writing feedback — requires longer AI outputs = more tokens. Must design this carefully.
- Student-to-paid conversion in India is notoriously low (<1% is common). Plan for a large free user base with a thin paid conversion layer.
